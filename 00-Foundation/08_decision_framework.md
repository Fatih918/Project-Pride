# PRIDE Decision Framework

**A Consistent Way to Make Decisions**

---

# Purpose

As PRIDE grows, thousands of technical and non-technical decisions will be made.

This framework exists to ensure that every significant decision remains consistent with the Foundation rather than personal preference.

The goal is not to eliminate disagreement.

The goal is to ensure that disagreement leads to better decisions.

When implementation conflicts with the Foundation,

the implementation should change.

Not the Foundation.

---

# Why This Framework Exists

Technology evolves.

People change.

Communities grow.

Different contributors will naturally have different opinions.

Rather than relying on authority, popularity, or seniority, PRIDE relies on principles.

This framework provides a consistent way to evaluate proposals, architectural changes, and long-term decisions.

---

# Decision Order

Every significant decision should be evaluated in the following order.

## 1. Foundation

Does this align with the Manifesto, Constitution, Vision, Mission, Core Values, Principles, and Governance?

If the answer is no,

the proposal should not proceed.

---

## 2. Human Impact

Does this improve the experience of people?

Does it reduce unnecessary complexity?

Does it preserve human control?

Technology exists to serve humanity.

Not the opposite.

---

## 3. Long-Term Sustainability

Will this still be the right decision years from now?

Does it increase technical debt?

Does it make future maintenance more difficult?

Short-term convenience should never outweigh long-term sustainability.

---

## 4. Core or Plugin?

Can this capability exist as a plugin?

If yes,

it should remain outside the Core unless there is exceptional architectural justification.

Stable Core.

Unlimited Extensions.

---

## 5. Replaceability

Does this introduce a dependency that cannot easily be replaced?

If yes,

the architecture should be reconsidered.

No technology should become irreplaceable.

---

## 6. Transparency

Will users understand what PRIDE is doing?

Can meaningful actions be explained?

Transparency builds trust.

---

## 7. Privacy

Does this require user data?

Is every piece of collected information necessary?

Can the same outcome be achieved with less data?

Privacy should always be respected.

---

## 8. Community

Will this decision make PRIDE easier or harder to maintain?

Will future contributors understand it?

Healthy ecosystems prioritize maintainability over cleverness.

---

## 9. Simplicity

Can this solution be made simpler?

Simple systems are easier to understand, maintain, and evolve.

---

## 10. Future Compatibility

Will this design remain useful if today's technologies disappear?

Architectures should outlive their implementations whenever practical.

---

# Decision Checklist

Before approving any significant proposal, ask:

✓ Does it respect the Foundation?

✓ Does it benefit people?

✓ Does it preserve simplicity?

✓ Does it maintain replaceability?

✓ Does it respect privacy?

✓ Does it improve maintainability?

✓ Does it encourage community contribution?

✓ Will future developers understand it?

If several answers are "No",

the proposal should be reconsidered.

---

# When Principles Conflict

No engineering decision will satisfy every principle perfectly.

Trade-offs are inevitable.

The objective is not perfection,

but the best long-term balance while remaining faithful to the Foundation.

When principles appear to conflict:

* Prioritize the Foundation.
* Prefer solutions that benefit people.
* Preserve simplicity whenever practical.
* Preserve replaceability whenever possible.
* Protect privacy by default.
* Favor long-term sustainability over short-term convenience.
* Document the reasoning behind the decision.

Every important compromise should be intentional, transparent, and explainable.

---

# Handling Disagreement

Disagreement is expected.

Healthy discussion strengthens the project.

When disagreement occurs:

1. Return to the Foundation.
2. Evaluate the proposal using this Decision Framework.
3. Focus on long-term sustainability rather than personal preference.
4. Document the reasoning behind the final decision.
5. Accept the outcome respectfully, even when opinions differ.

Better ideas should always prevail.

Not louder voices.

Not seniority.

Not popularity.

---

# Closing Statement

This framework does not exist to slow innovation.

It exists to protect meaningful innovation.

Good decisions create good architecture.

Good architecture creates lasting platforms.

Every decision made today becomes part of the future that others will inherit.

Choose accordingly.

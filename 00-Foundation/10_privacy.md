# PRIDE Privacy

**Privacy Is a Fundamental Right, Not a Premium Feature.**

---

# Purpose

Privacy is one of the permanent principles of PRIDE.

People should never be required to sacrifice their privacy in exchange for functionality.

This document defines the principles that guide how PRIDE collects, stores, processes, and protects information.

These principles are intended to remain stable regardless of future technologies or implementation details.

---

# Privacy as a Right

Privacy is a fundamental human right.

It is not a feature.

It is not an upgrade.

It is not a premium subscription.

Every person deserves meaningful control over their own information.

PRIDE exists to protect that principle.

---

# Ownership

Information created by a person belongs to that person.

This includes, but is not limited to:

* Personal data.
* Conversations.
* Memories.
* Workflows.
* Preferences.
* Files.
* Configurations.

PRIDE may process information to provide its services.

It does not claim ownership of that information.

---

# User Control

People should remain in control of their own data.

Whenever practical, they should be able to:

* View it.
* Export it.
* Modify it.
* Delete it.
* Decide where it is stored.

Technology should adapt to people.

Not the other way around.

---

# Data Minimization

PRIDE should collect only the information that is necessary to provide the requested functionality.

Information should never be collected simply because it may become useful in the future.

Less data collected means less data that can be exposed, misused, or lost.

---

# Transparency

People should understand:

* What information is used.
* Why it is used.
* When it is used.
* Where it is stored.
* Who can access it.

Privacy cannot exist without transparency.

---

# Local First

Whenever technically practical, PRIDE should allow information to remain under the user's control through local processing and storage.

Cloud services may provide additional capabilities.

They should complement local operation rather than replace it whenever practical.

---

# Consent

Meaningful data processing should be based on informed consent.

Consent should be clear.

Specific.

Understandable.

Easy to withdraw.

Silence should never be interpreted as permission.

---

# Security

Privacy depends upon security.

PRIDE should encourage responsible protection of information through secure design and implementation.

Security is not optional.

It is an essential part of protecting privacy.

---

# Third-Party Services

People should know when information is shared with third-party services.

Whenever practical, PRIDE should clearly distinguish between local processing and external services.

Users should remain free to choose the services they trust.

---

# Privacy by Design

Privacy should be considered from the beginning of every design decision.

It should never become an afterthought.

Every new capability should ask:

"Can this be achieved while collecting less information?"

Whenever the answer is yes,

the more privacy-preserving approach should be preferred.

---

# Long-Term Commitment

The growth of PRIDE should never reduce the privacy of its users.

New capabilities should strengthen privacy whenever possible.

Convenience should never become an excuse for unnecessary data collection.

---

# Closing Statement

Privacy is not an obstacle to innovation.

It is one of the conditions that makes trustworthy innovation possible.

PRIDE believes that people should benefit from intelligent technology without surrendering ownership of their information.

Technology should earn trust.

Never demand it.
